3. Code Quality Review

Resolved: Facade concurrency is guarded synchronously

Why it matters: The facade no longer waits until an OtaClient exists before reporting an update in progress.

Impact: Two callers cannot both pass the guard and begin manipulating OTA session state.

Resolved: BLE notification lifecycle is explicit

Why it matters: Notification enable/disable calls now flow through BleRepository, making cleanup testable and consistent.

Impact: This reduces stale notification risk after success, cancellation, failure, or disconnect cleanup.

High: Raw progress 100 is still treated as terminal

Why it is a problem: ESP-IDF reports transfer progress using rounded percentages, including 100 after the last data chunk at lib/src/protocol/espidf_ota_protocol.dart:120-125. The facade closes the public stream on any 100 at lib/src/esp32_ota_package.dart:119-124.

Impact: A final control-read failure or device-side hash mismatch can be hidden from listeners because the public progress stream may already be closed as a success-looking 100.

Recommended fix: Reserve public terminal 100 for confirmed OtaClient success, or move to a typed event stream with separate progress, success, failure, and cancellation events.

Medium: Facade cleanup contract remains unclear

Why it is a problem: The facade closes its public stream at terminal values, but keeps _activeClient and the progress subscription until explicit dispose or a later update setup.

Impact: Consumer expectations around "self-dispose" and reuse after terminal states remain easy to misunderstand.

Recommended fix: Either perform facade cleanup after client.run completes, or revise documentation so ownership and reuse rules are explicit.
